Traditional methods for solving differential equations, such as finite element or finite difference methods, often require discretization of the domain and can be computationally expensive for high-dimensional problems. Physical Informed Neural Networks (PiNNs) propose an alternative approach where the solution is approximated by a neural network that is trained not only on data but also by enforcing the underlying physical laws via the differential equations.
The overall loss function $\mathcal{L}$ used is composed by the sum of two terms, which are the data loss $\mathcal{L}_{data}$ and the physics loss $\mathcal{L}_{phy}$. 
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}(\theta) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}(\theta),
\end{equation}
The first term defines the discrepancy between the prediction of the network and the observed data, while the second one specifies the correct modeling of the differential equation.
Usually $\mathcal{L}_{data}$ is define as the mean squared error between the prediction and the ground truth, while $\mathcal{L}_{phy}$ is defined using the residual of the differential equation.

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}(\theta) &= \frac{1}{N_u} \sum_{i=1}^{N_u} \left| u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_i, t_i) - u^{\text{obs}}_i \right|^2 \\
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}(\theta) &= \frac{1}{N_r} \sum_{j=1}^{N_r} \left| \mathcal{N}[u_{\theta}](\mathbf{x}_j, t_j) \right|^2,
\end{align}

As previously stated, we have used a bottom-up approach by first testing this approach to a Unicycle. This robot is characterized by simple kinematic equations. Its configuration vector is composed by the cartesian position $(x,y)$ and its orientation angle $\theta$, while the control inputs are the driving velocity $v$ and the steering velocity $w$.
We have used the PiNN to predict the next state $q_{k+1}$ of the robot given the current state, the control input given by the MPC and the time instant. Over a small time instant we have chosen to use Euler integration to do the approximation of the next state: $q_{k+1} = q_{k} + \Delta t \cdot f(q_{k}, u_{k})$, where the dynamic function is defined as follows.

\begin{equation}
    f(q_{k}, u_{k}) = 
    \begin{cases}
        {x_{k+1}} = v_{k} \cos(\theta_{k}) \\
        {y_{k+1}} = v_{k} \sin(\theta_{k}) \\
        {\theta_{k+1}} = w_{k} \\
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
